# Compare Lenovo T14s Generation 3 laptop models, Rank Best to last

For your R/Python coding and light local-LLM use, my **ranking** is: AMD Ryzen 7 first, AMD Ryzen 5 second, then Intel configurations. This is a buying-preference ranking at comparable RAM, condition, and price—not a claim that AMD wins every benchmark. The tested Ryzen 7 model excelled in sustained multicore work, while Intel did better in some single-core tasks.[^1_1]

## T14s Gen 3: best to last

| Rank | Processor configuration | Assessment |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | AMD Ryzen 7 PRO 6850U | Best overall target: 8 cores / 16 threads, Radeon 680M, and LPDDR5-6400 memory. Particularly attractive for sustained parallel workloads. [^1_2][^1_1] |
| 2 | AMD Ryzen 5 PRO 6650U | My value-oriented alternative: 6 cores / 12 threads, Radeon 660M, and the same LPDDR5-6400 memory platform. Choose it when meaningfully cheaper or better equipped with RAM. [^1_2] |
| 3 | Intel Core i7-1255U / i7-1265U | Good alternatives for everyday development. Both have 2 performance cores + 8 efficiency cores / 12 threads; Intel’s single-core strengths can matter for workloads that do not parallelize. [^1_3][^1_1] |
| 4 | Intel Core i5-1235U / i5-1245U | Sensible budget alternatives. Same core/thread layout as the i7 U-series, with lower maximum clocks. The tested i5-1235U was competitive in everyday and single-core work. [^1_3][^1_1] |
| 5 | Intel P-series: i7-1280P, i7-1270P, i7-1260P, i5-1250P, i5-1240P | Conditional choice rather than my default. They offer more performance cores, but that alone does not establish better sustained performance in this thin chassis. The i7-1280P was a special-bid EMEA option. [^1_3][^1_1] |

The P-series placement is not “slowest CPU.” It means I would not pay extra for one without evidence that the specific configuration performs better for your workload. The available review comparison does not test every T14s processor option.[^1_1]

## RAM can change the order

Both AMD and Intel T14s Gen 3 models have soldered RAM, with 8, 16, or 32 GB configurations and no memory slots. You cannot upgrade RAM later.[^1_2][^1_3]

For your purchase, I would prioritize:

1. 32 GB RAM.
2. Ryzen 7 over Ryzen 5 when RAM and price are comparable.
3. A good screen and overall condition.
4. SSD capacity—the machine has a separate M.2 2280 SSD slot.[^1_3][^1_2]

Consequently, my preference would be a Ryzen 5 / 32 GB over a Ryzen 7 / 16 GB if memory capacity is the limiting factor in your work.

## Configuration to look for

My preferred specification is:

- Ryzen 7 PRO 6850U.
- 32 GB RAM.
- 1920 × 1200, 400-nit low-power IPS display.
- 512 GB or larger SSD. These are all listed factory options.[^1_2]

The 400-nit panel is a useful upgrade over the basic 300-nit screen: Lenovo specifies 100% sRGB coverage versus 45% NTSC on the basic panel. Both AMD and Intel were offered with Linux/Ubuntu, though that is not a guarantee that every optional feature works under Debian.[^1_3][^1_2]

One connectivity distinction: AMD has one USB4 port plus one USB-C 3.2 Gen 2 port; Intel has two Thunderbolt 4 ports. Intel is worth considering if a particular Thunderbolt dock or peripheral is central to your setup.[^1_2][^1_3]

# For my purposes, what are the disadvantages or cons of Lenovo P14s Gen 3 vs T14s Gen3, if the other specifications are similar? AMD chip, RAM, etc.

For your Debian, R/Python coding, and light local-LLM use, the P14s Gen 3 AMD has **few** important disadvantages compared with a similarly equipped T14s Gen 3 AMD. The main compromises are portability, battery capacity, and the lack of USB4—not an inherently weaker CPU or memory platform. Both use Ryzen PRO 6000 U-series processors and LPDDR5-6400 memory.[^2_1][^2_2][^2_3]

## Main disadvantages of the P14s

| Difference | P14s Gen 3 AMD | T14s Gen 3 AMD | Importance for you |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Size and weight | About 17.9 mm thick; Lenovo’s published starting weights vary by configuration/document, roughly 1.28–1.4 kg | About 16.6 mm thick; starts around 1.22 kg | Minor unless you carry it frequently. [^2_2][^2_4][^2_3][^2_5] |
| Battery capacity | 39.3 Wh or 52.5 Wh | 57 Wh | Modest disadvantage with the 52.5 Wh battery; more substantial with 39.3 Wh. Since battery hours are not your priority, this need not decide the purchase. [^2_2][^2_3] |
| USB-C capability | Two USB-C 3.2 Gen 2 ports; no USB4 | One USB4 port, plus one USB-C 3.2 Gen 2 port | Mostly matters for high-speed peripherals or particular docking setups, rather than ordinary coding. [^2_2][^2_6] |

Battery capacity alone does not establish actual runtime: screen, power settings, workload, and the condition of a used battery also matter.[^2_7]

## What is not a disadvantage

- No special RAM penalty: both have soldered LPDDR5-6400 RAM with no expansion slots, up to 32 GB. The AMD P14s does not offer the RAM-upgrade advantage sometimes associated with workstation models.[^2_2][^2_3]
- No automatic coding-performance penalty: the CPU families and memory specifications overlap. Actual sustained performance can differ with cooling and power settings, but those specifications do not justify calling the P14s slower.[^2_3][^2_1]
- No dedicated NVIDIA GPU complication in the AMD configuration: it uses integrated Radeon graphics. Its “workstation” designation emphasizes professional-application certification, not a separate GPU that you need for your work.[^2_4][^2_5]

## Linux and buying recommendation

I would not rank the P14s below the T14s for Debian reliability solely because it is a P-series machine. However, the sources here do not establish that their Debian behavior is identical. The T14s has model-specific Linux documentation covering firmware updates and power-management configuration; that is useful evidence, not proof that the P14s is worse.[^2_8]

My recommendation: at the same price, RAM, screen, and condition, favor the T14s for its lighter chassis, larger battery, and USB4. If the P14s is meaningfully cheaper—or gives you 32 GB instead of 16 GB—I would favor that configuration for your purposes rather than pay a premium for the T14s chassis. Both platforms offer the same memory speed, and neither allows a later RAM upgrade.[^2_2][^2_3]

Check the actual display, too: a P14s listing can have the basic 300-nit, 45% NTSC panel, while a T14s listing may have the better 400-nit, 100% sRGB low-power panel. That is a configuration difference, not an unavoidable P14s drawback.[^2_5][^2_9]

# Laptop requirements and options (researched 2026-10-18)

## Requirement

- Used (not new), eBay availability matters.
- 14" business-class, AMD, 32 GB RAM.
- Runs Linux well (current HP runs Linux "but not very well" — avoid HP).
- First choice was T14s / P14s Gen 3 AMD, but 32 GB units are scarce used.

## Bottom line

No other brand beats Lenovo here for a used AMD business machine, because **Dell never made an AMD
Latitude/Precision** (AMD only arrived with the 2025+ "Dell Pro" line, so nothing used yet). Fix = change
**Lenovo model/generation**, and/or buy **socketed-RAM** machines so you are not hunting a rare factory
32 GB unit.

## 1. Same family, different model/generation — better used supply

| Model | CPU | RAM | Why | Used eBay seen (Oct 2026) |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **T14 Gen 3 AMD** (non-s) | Ryzen 7 PRO 6850U/6650U | soldered LPDDR5 up to 32 GB | Same platform as T14s G3; corporate-fleet volume → far more listings; thicker, bigger battery (52.5 vs 57 Wh) | 32 GB units ~$500-630 |
| **T14 Gen 4 AMD** / **T14s Gen 4 AMD** | Ryzen 7 PRO 7840U | soldered, 32 GB | One gen newer, Zen4 + Radeon 780M (~2x the 680M, better for local LLM), excellent Linux | ~$439-795 for 32 GB |
| **P14s Gen 4 AMD** | Ryzen 7 PRO 7840U | soldered, 32 GB | Workstation twin of T14 G4 AMD; same specs | ~$700+ |
| **X13 Gen 3 AMD** | Ryzen 6000 | soldered, 32 GB | 13.3", lighter, better base screen (72% gamut) than T14s | ~$310-599 |
| **X13 Gen 4 AMD** | Ryzen 7040 | soldered, 32 GB | Gen4 version of above | ~$349+ |

Escalating to **Gen 4 (7840U)** is a spec upgrade for this use (much stronger iGPU / AVX-512 for light
local LLM), not a compromise.

## 2. Socketed RAM — sidesteps the 32 GB scarcity entirely

| Model | RAM | Linux | Used | Trade-off |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **ThinkPad L14 Gen 3 AMD** | 2x DDR4 SODIMM, up to 64 GB | Ubuntu/Linux listed | ~$200-320 | Ryzen 5000 (Zen3), Vega iGPU → weaker for LLM |
| **Framework Laptop 13 AMD** (7840U) | 2x DDR5 SODIMM, up to 64 GB | best-in-class, repairable/upgradable | 32 GB seen ~$700-900 | thin used pool, sells fast, 61 Wh, no TrackPoint |
| ThinkPad T480/T480s | 2x SODIMM 32-64 GB | legendary | ~$150-300 | 8th-gen Intel, ~half the CPU, weak iGPU — budget only |

Strategy: buy a **16 GB** L14/Framework and add a 16 GB stick — much easier than finding factory 32 GB.

## 3. Ruled out

- **Dell** — no AMD Latitude/Precision ever; Intel Latitudes have documented Linux defects (7430 UI lag,
  no S3 sleep). New "Dell Pro" AMD is 2025+ ⇒ no used market yet.
- **HP EliteBook 845 G9/G10 AMD** — hardware is fine and mostly Linux-OK, but there are documented
  suspend / BIOS-update / Wi-Fi breakages (HP support, ArchWiki, GitHub). Matches the existing doubt — skip.
- **System76, Tuxedo, Slimbook, Star Labs, Purism** — new-only; no used market.

## 4. Practical tactics

- eBay saved searches + alerts: `T14s Gen 4 AMD 32GB`, `T14 Gen 3 AMD 32GB`, `X13 Gen 3 AMD 32GB`,
  `Framework 13 AMD 7840U`.
- Prefer 16 GB socketed models and add RAM yourself.
- Lenovo Outlet / refurb resellers for factory 32 GB.
- Check the ArchWiki page for the exact model before buying (that is where Linux evidence lives).

## Recommended order

1. T14 Gen 4 AMD 32 GB
2. T14s Gen 4 AMD 32 GB
3. T14 Gen 3 AMD 32 GB
4. P14s Gen 4 AMD
5. Fallback: L14 Gen 3 AMD or Framework 13 AMD with self-added RAM
