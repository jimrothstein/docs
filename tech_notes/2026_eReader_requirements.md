## Durability and the "3–4 years, no repairs" requirement

- **Age is the main risk, not the screen.** E-ink panels last a long time; **batteries and USB-C ports** are what fail. Boox reports no battery-cycle data, so ask the seller for real-world battery life and charge habits.
- **Buy 2023 or newer** (Android 12+) to keep both hardware and app support inside your horizon: Go 10.3 Gen 1 (2024), Go 10.3 Gen II (2026), Note Air3 B/W (2023). Prefer 2022+ over 2020–21.
- **Android version is also a shelf life.** Android 10/11 devices still run Google Drive and KOReader today, but they will age out of new app support sooner than Android 12–15 units. [^e11]
- **Boox has no consumer repair program in most markets**, so treat a used purchase as unrepairable: buy from the **official BOOX eBay store** (refurbished units with warranty) or a seller who accepts returns, and avoid units with visible screen cracks or case separation. [^e4][^e17]

## Stylus — what is the benefit if you never write, draw or sketch?

Almost none. The screen is touch-based, so the pen is optional.

Remaining (minor) uses for a reader:
- precise **highlighting of PDF text**, and text selection for search / dictionary / copy
- dragging the **crop-margin** box and tapping tiny footnote or citation links in dense math PDFs (more accurate than a fingertip)
- keeps fingerprint smudges off the screen

Not applicable to you: handwritten notes, sketches, margin ink, handwriting OCR. (Your earlier `000_eBook_reader.md` wish — "read handwriting (incl. handwritten equations)" — is the only case where a pen would matter, and you have since listed writing/drawing under *no need*.)

Practical: a replacement **BOOX Pen2 Pro is ~$80** (eraser + magnetic); the stock Pen Plus has no eraser [^e20]. Prefer the cheaper pen-less listing, but do not reject a good bundle that includes one.

## What "change fonts and margins" really means for PDFs

Fixed-layout PDFs **cannot** change font size. Your real controls are:

1. **Crop margins** (BOOX NeoReader: Format panel → crop/zoom/margin) — removes white borders, making the text bigger on the same screen [^e13].
2. **Zoom + split-column / article mode** (NeoReader Navigation panel) — good for two-column papers [^e13].
3. **Reflow** (KOReader, or NeoReader's reflow) — re-extracts text so font/size/margins can change, but it **garbles equations and figures** in math PDFs [^e12].
4. **KOReader** adds arbitrary margins, external fonts, per-document settings, and an e-ink-tuned dark mode [^e12].

Consequence for buying: since reflow is unreliable on math, **resolution is what buys readability** — which is why the 300 ppi Go 10.3 leads, and why 227 ppi devices put hard-to-read subscripts on screen. Cropping the margin gives you maybe 10–15% more text size; 300 ppi gives you legible subscripts at any size. (KOReader's dark mode is a software feature, so it does not replace the front light you don't need anyway.)

## Caveats

1. **eBay Boox pricing is erratic** — sellers frequently ask more than Boox's own new price, and color models pollute the search results [^e17]. Filter by *Sold*, and exclude any listing with "C" in the model name.
2. **Android 12 is the sensible floor** for a 3–4 year horizon (Go 10.3 Gen 1, Note Air3 B/W); Android 15 (Go 10.3 Gen II) is better.
3. **No front light is a non-issue** given your "optional — do not see need to read in dark" note, which is exactly why the Go 10.3 Gen 1 is rank 1 rather than the Lumi.
4. **64 GB is plenty for PDFs**; microSD only matters if you want a full mirror of your Calibre library on-device.
5. **Used-device checks**: real-world battery life, ghosting/afterimages, dead pixels, USB-C port wear, case separation, and whether the folio is included.
6. **Android can be distracting.** Sign into Drive only, hide the Play Store, and stay in the Library / KOReader. [^e11]

## Practical setup (Calibre, Drive, grouping)

1. **Transfer** — Calibre over USB (Android shows up via MTP) or wirelessly through Calibre's content server / Calibre-Web. [^e11]
2. **Browse on device** — install **KOReader** (Play Store / F-Droid). It browses Calibre-Web through OPDS, reads PDF/EPUB/DjVu, and handles crop, reflow, margins, and collections synced from Calibre tags. [^e12]
3. **Grouping** — either Calibre tags/series → KOReader collections, or plain folders in internal storage shown by the BOOX Library app. Both satisfy "math, calculus, LLM, mcp, health, …".
4. **Google Drive** — the Drive app for on-demand download; FolderSync / Syncthing to keep a synced library folder.
5. **Zotero** — has an Android app and runs on BOOX (impossible on Kobo/Kindle/Supernote/reMarkable). [^e11]
